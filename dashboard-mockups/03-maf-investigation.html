Open investigation

Is the mass air flow sensor under-reporting airflow on the Mazda 6?

Fuel economy dropped from ~34/~24 MPG (route/tank) to ~29/~19 on the same known route. Four diagnostic hypotheses from the original blueprint have now been checked against 34 drives of real fuel-trim data — one of them is holding up, and it isn't the octane/timing-retard theory that looked promising early on.

!
Combined fuel trim (STFT+LTFT) has run positive on 32 of 34 drives since Long Term Fuel Trim data became reliable on Aug 7 — consistently +2% to +9%, present at every driving phase, not concentrated at idle. That shape is the MAF-under-reporting signature, not a vacuum/PCV leak (which spikes hard at idle specifically).

Idle-vs-moving split averages +5.4% at idle vs. +4.2% moving across these drives — a mild gradient, not the sharp +10-25%+ idle-only spike a real vacuum leak produces. This is exactly the pattern the project's own diagnostic playbook uses to tell the two apart.

Signal 3 of 3

DriveTrace's MPG estimate runs above the dash — in the direction a low MAF reading predicts

DriveTrace's fuel-burned estimate is entirely MAF-derived (airflow × assumed stoichiometric ratio). If the sensor under-reports airflow, this estimate under-reports fuel burned, which over-reports MPG. Two drives have a driver-logged dash reading to check that against:

Ruled out

Octane-driven timing retard

The SKYACTIV-G 2.5T retards timing under knock on lower-octane fuel, a real, mundane, non-mechanical way to lose MPG on its own. Checked directly:

Knock Retard, mean across drives
Near zero on every drive with data. Brief spikes only, never sustained — no chronic knock mitigation on current fuel.
What this doesn't rule out
Subtler base-map effect
Rules out the dramatic version of this hypothesis only. Fuel trim remains the stronger, more consistent signal to act on.

Next step

What the data supports doing next

Clean or replace the MAF sensor, then log a comparable drive.

Cheap (~10 minutes, isopropyl or MAF-specific cleaner) and directly testable: if combined trim drops back toward 0% on the next few drives, that's confirmation, not correlation. This is no longer "a plausible theory that can't be checked" — it's a real, repeatable, 34-drive-confirmed signal, cleanly distinguished from a vacuum leak by its own shape.
